DataGuardian: Safeguarding Your Business's Digital Heartbeat

Meet our customer DataGuardian—anonymized here to honor confidentiality agreements. Their core offering? A secure vault for Salesforce CRM data, the diary of every customer interaction. But their next chapter was just beginning:Turning all that data into clear, confident decisions through actionable insights.

Unlocking the Vault of Data’s Potential

First up: sales data. The goal was to help their clients’ sales teams spot the most promising leads—fast.
From “where do I even look?” to “let’s go close this deal.”

That’s where TensorIoT stepped in. I joined as the sole UX designer, tasked with translating complex ML predictions and raw Salesforce data into a dashboard that was clear, useful, and actually enjoyable to use.
Not just a visualization—a strategic tool to drive smarter, faster decisions for sales teams.

The QuickSight Quest: Design Discovery to Sign-Off In Two Weeks

In our initial discovery and design sprint, my knack for asking incisive questions and bringing customer ideas to life steered the project, ensuring smooth and successful progress. Here are some highlights:

  1. Kicking Off Design Discovery

    To start, I tapped into our sales team's knowledge about DataGuardian's goals and challenges to shape some initial design sketches.

    Before our first working session, I mapped out a rough dashboard based on Sales insights and contract requirements. It wasn’t polished—but it was just enough to align stakeholders quickly and turn vague goals into something buildable.

    Then came the stakeholder kickoff.
    I dug into user needs, business goals, and what success should actually look like. Highlights from that session:

    • Our users? B2B sales reps and managers—especially at smaller firms where every relationship counts.
    • Their current workflow? Mostly vibes. They guess which deals will close based on gut feel.
    • The ask? A dashboard that translates predictions into dollar signs and looks sharp enough to help sell the product itself.
  2. Creative Solution Crafting

    During our initial discovery call, I realized my sketches weren't compelling or "cool" enough for DataGuardian’s needs. So, I revisited the designs, drawing inspiration from QuickSight's DemoCentral and discussing available data with our developers to brainstorm new graph concepts.

    Potential insights for users
    1. Allows sales teams to quickly identify high-value and high-potential deals, focusing their attention where it can have the most impact.
    2. Enables identification of high-value opportunities that require immediate focus and lower-value ones that can be nurtured over time.
    3. Simplifies understanding of win-loss ratios within a sales portfolio, useful for quick assessment of sales outcomes.
    1. Allows sellers to easily see the proportion of deals in each win-likelihood category, focusing efforts where there’s a higher chance of success.
    2. Tracks individual or team performance across different stages, spotting trends in sales success or areas for improvement.
    3. Illustrates the cumulative performance of sales teams across various categories, useful for assessing overall sales health.
    1. Visualizes the distribution and intensity of sales activities, highlighting areas of high engagement and potential bottlenecks within the sales pipeline.
    2. Provides a clear ranking of sales reps, motivating underperformers and acknowledging top performers for strategy optimization.
  3. When your users sit one Slack message away...

    There wasn’t time on the books for research. No one asked for it. In the past, I’d even been told not to bother. But in this case, our sales team was the target audience—and just a message away. So I reached out.

    I met with our rep and the sales manager to understand how they identify high-potential opportunities, coach their teams, and evaluate strategy. A short conversation, a big unlock.

  4. Achieving Final Sign-off

    Through several more cycles of discovery, sketch iterations, and review with both the customer and developers, I honed in on graph choices that narrated a well-rounded and compelling story, enriched by insights from my user research.

    User Insight #1

    Simplicity Wins: Individual sellers, pressed for time, prefer straightforward visuals. Complex graphs like heat maps lose them, while simpler ones like pie charts hit the mark.

    Design Decisions

    This pie chart provides clear, quick insights, allowing sales reps to see their win and loss potential at a glance. By showing the likely revenue from potential deals, it helps sellers prioritize their efforts and focus on strategies that maximize earnings and meet sales targets efficiently.

    User Insight #2

    Competitive Spirit: Showing where they stand compared to others fuels individual sellers' drive, with visualizations of rankings pushing them to excel.

    Design Decisions

    This leaderboard illuminates each seller's standing with color-coded predictions of close-won likelihood, sparking a drive to not just climb the ranks but also focus efforts on the most winnable deals to improve their standing and the company's bottom line.

    User Insight #3

    Effort vs. Reward: Sellers boost returns with minimal work by pinpointing opportunities that yield big rewards for the least effort, letting them chase more deals.

    Design Decisions

    The scatter plot guides sellers to high-value, undecided deals at the top-middle—the sweet spot where little effort gets big wins. Deals at the bottom offer less reward, while those on the far sides rarely change, making the center the key target.

    User Insight #4

    Big-Picture Concerns: Sales managers focus on the overall health of their team by ensuring adequate support, recognition, training, and strong performance forecasts.

    Design Decisions

    The ‘Sales Performance Trajectory’ graph shows monthly wins and losses to help managers forecast and gauge pipeline health.

    ‘Pipeline Potential’ displays a heatmap of opportunities by closing chance, value, and stage, letting managers adjust tactics and training when deals stray.

    DataGuardian made one thing clear: the dashboard needed to look sleek enough to help sell the product—without sacrificing clarity.

    From Basic to Branded
    My first pass prioritized clarity, using a red-yellow-green palette that was easy to scan—but looked a little too… elementary.

    So I reworked it using their brand colors. The result? A sleek, elegant design that felt polished and perfectly at home in the rest of their ecosystem.

    Sign-Off, Full Speed Ahead
    By the end of our two-week sprint, I secured design sign-off—confirming that the dashboard’s structure, flow, and content hit the mark. With that green light, the dev team could charge ahead with confidence, no surprise revisions in sight.

Design-to-Dev, in Real Life

As designs turned into dashboards, I worked closely with developers to bridge the gap between vision and reality. When QuickSight’s limitations clashed with our ideal UI, I didn’t just toss the designs over the fence—I rolled up my sleeves.

I led frequent handoff reviews, flagged mismatches early, and partnered with the devs to rethink tricky components without losing sight of user needs or timelines. Together, we made the polished, branded experience feel effortless—even if it wasn’t.

The Big Reveal: A Tool That Sees the Future 🔮

The unveiling of the final PoC dashboard was a momentous occasion, met with enthusiasm and accolades from DataGuardian. Their expectations were not only met but exceeded, leading to immediate discussions for further collaborations between our two companies. The success of the dashboard underscored my ability to turn complex data into an intuitive, engaging tool for decision-making.

Charting the Course: Lessons from the UX Voyage

Mastering an unfamiliar tool and navigating the new, detailed world of sales pipelines proved to be a rewarding challenge. This experience showcased my adaptability and dedication to growing my UX design skills, while also highlighting my quick ability to learn and innovate in new areas.

The success of my designs for this project sparked notable changes within our organization and beyond:

  1. My work with QuickSight led our company to adopt it for more projects, enhancing our visualization tools.
  2. The success of this project prompted my company to replace an costly, custom internal tool with a QuickSight dashboard, cutting weekly costs by over $600.
  3. My dashboard designs drew attention from Amazon's QuickSight designers, resulting in an invite for my team to beta test their new Figma library.
  4. After asking me to showcase this solution company-wide, our CEO praised the user research I conducted on the project, urging the entire company to pursue similar efforts on other projects.
